#### Narrative:
We tackled again the conflicts we had about rewarding Latam Q2 work and then moved to discuss on how we approach planning the Q3 activities and budget aside from the current active ASI Growth project

A related discussion considered how the Program should identify and prioritize improvements to its own tools. Participants generally supported collecting ideas, but disagreed about whether formal proposals would improve prioritization or create bureaucracy, competition, feature creep and additional governance work.


#### Discussion Points:
- LatAm Q2 reward previous blockers against the LatAm reward had been withdrawn and we reassessed the reward amount and reasons of rewarding Latam for their work
- The continuation of regional outreach iniatives was discussed and related proposal was also shared.

The following possible models were discussed:
Fund established regional guilds separately from the regional initiatives allocation.
Use the regional initiatives allocation primarily for new, one-off, or clearly scoped projects.
Treat regional work as a project with defined outputs and impact measures.
Treat regional guilds as ongoing workgroups with recurring rewards.
or use a hybrid model of above.

It was noted that:
recurring funding is essential community infrastructure.
A concern was raised that small, fragmented rewards are unlikely to sustain regional communities and may create disproportionate administrative work.
- We briefly touched the idea of using reserves to prop up regional outreach activities
- We also discussed about non-project spending and its budget for tooling related initatives

Arguments supporting an open feature proposal process included:

allowing the community to identify needed improvements before tools are built;
making development priorities more transparent;
reducing the current pattern of building first and reporting later;
creating a shared pipeline of opportunities;
allowing proposals to remain short and lightweight;
supporting capability-based funding.

Concerns included:

excessive administrative work;
turning feature ideas into competing funding proposals;
distracting from core infrastructure;
encouraging feature creep;
sending operational product decisions unnecessarily to the Governance Workgroup;
creating a process that is too slow for small improvements.

One suggested compromise was to collect feature requests directly inside the relevant product or tool, while using a separate prioritization process rather than treating every request as a governance proposal or bounty.
